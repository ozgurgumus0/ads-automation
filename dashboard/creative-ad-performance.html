Campaign Breakdown

All channels, sourced from raw main_metrics — filter to one channel below. Cohort columns (Lead Cohort → CR) come from New_Data’s “UTM Campaign” column: every lead tagged with that campaign, whatever creative it came from. They used to be summed from the campaign’s creatives, which counted a creative once per campaign it ran in, which inflated channel totals many times over (fixed). Rows sourced this way carry a small utm marker. A campaign the column doesn’t name shows “—” rather than a creative-summed guess, and its leads appear on that channel’s “(Unattributed cohort)” row (marker rest) together with campaigns since renamed or retired, campaigns excluded from main_metrics (CAMPAIGN_NAME_EXCLUDE_RULES), unexpanded campaign macros and leads with no campaign recorded — so each channel’s total here reconciles with Channel Breakdown / Weekly Cohort Totals instead of reading low. Share of each channel’s cohort that lands on real campaign rows: TikTok 94%, Youtube 84%, PMax 84%, Google Search 74%, Display 72%, Meta 69%, Outbrain 75%. This page is scoped to the section you are in: in Outbrain mode it shows Outbrain campaigns only, and in the main dashboard it shows every channel except Outbrain, so the two are never mixed. A campaign renamed since its ads were built keeps the OLD label in its UTMs, so its earlier leads arrive under a name main_metrics no longer has; where the live name extends the old one (Campaign_X::A → ::A_High/::A_Low) those leads are moved back onto the row, capped by its Leads-minus-Lead-Cohort gap and flagged utm+. A UTM Campaign that is a bare campaign ID is resolved through main_metrics’ own Campaign ID column. One caveat on contracts: campaign rows read pre-archive-cutoff data from the live New_Data tab (the archive copy has no UTM Campaign column), and the live tab is fresher — a contract signed after the archive snapshot shows here but not in the channel total, which is why a channel can read one contract above Channel Breakdown. Unique Contracts is the one column still sourced from creatives (Lead_Contract_Cohort has no campaign column): a creative’s unique contracts are split across the campaigns it ran in by spend share, so it is an estimate rather than observed, and the conversions whose creative can’t be matched — all of Google Search’s, which has no creative-level attribution at all — sit on the “(Unattributed cohort)” row so the column still totals the channel’s real figure.
